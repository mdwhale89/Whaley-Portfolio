Assignment 1: Rotoscoping & Character Asset Creation

Kinematic ASL Translation & Frame-by-Frame Vectorization

The objective of this production was to translate the expressive, kinetic movement of American Sign Language (ASL) into a vectorized character asset. Utilizing the rotoscoping technique, I meticulously traced my full self-introduction video in Photoshop, frame-by-frame.

This rigorous process required isolating key elements—including the precise geometry of hand signs and facial expressions—into independent layers. By managing these nested layers and focusing on the kinematic flow of communication, I was able to engineer a complex performance trace that maintains the clarity and intent of the original ASL message while achieving a bespoke, hand-drawn aesthetic.

What is Rotoscoping?

Traditional Craft in Modern Digital Pipelines

Rotoscoping is a traditional animation technique where a creator traces over live-action film footage, frame-by-frame, to produce realistic action. By using an original video as a reference, an animator can capture the subtle geometry of motion—such as the complex hand shapes used in American Sign Language—that would be difficult to recreate from scratch.

Assignment 2: Narrative Synthesis & Soundscape Design

Live-Action Staging, Asset Harmonization & Comedic Timing

For the production of Judith Viorst’s "Mother Doesn’t Want a Dog," my goal was to create a unified cinematic world by blending personal performance with curated digital assets. This project required a dual-focus strategy: managing the unpredictable humor of live subjects—my mother and my dog, Joker—and conducting a rigorous search for external footage that could seamlessly expand the story.

To hit the specific comedic beats of the poem, I had to engineer a "performance" from Joker, who is highly responsive to verbal commands. I directed my mother to "yell" and point at him silently, using only physical gestures. This "Silent Command" strategy bypassed Joker’s training, allowing me to capture his natural, inquisitive expression as he stared back with a toy in his mouth—providing the perfect visual "What?" to match the narrative.

While the footage of my family provided the emotional heart of the piece, the rest of the production was a study in Strategic Asset Curation. Finding online clips that harmonized with my personal 1080p footage was an arduous process of visual matching. I evaluated hundreds of external assets to find clips that shared a similar "narrative energy" and lighting profile, ensuring that the transition between my living room and the digital stock felt like a single, unified world.

Assignment 3: Asset Fabrication & Layered Environments

Hand-Drawn Architecture, Modular Systems & Hierarchical Animation

Every environment is built on a foundation of original, hand-drawn asset architecture. Moving away from stock libraries, I illustrated every modular component from scratch—ranging from architectural structures like the windmill and chimney to organic characters and atmospheric layers. This bespoke approach ensured a cohesive visual style across all layers, providing a unique aesthetic that is both intentional and technically optimized for the animation pipeline.

The challenge of this production was two-fold: first, illustrating a complete library of bespoke environmental and character assets by hand, and second, engineering a tiered animation system where those assets functioned both as independent moving parts and as cohesive elements of a 40-second narrative.

Beyond the primary camera movements, I isolated specific components—such as character limbs and atmospheric effects—to create secondary animations within the main timeline. This process required a deep understanding of parent-child relationships and rhythmic timing to ensure that these "micro-motions" stayed synchronized with the "macro-motion" of the overall scene.
